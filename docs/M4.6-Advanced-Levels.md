# M4.6 — Advanced puzzle levels

This is the original six-level implementation report. The current FeedbackCampaign
has eight levels after [M4.6 Phase B](M4.6-Phase-B-Master-Levels.md), which preserves
these layouts and adds Chain Reaction and Final Lockdown with Editor keys 7 and 8.

Implemented against Unity 6000.3.8f1, URP 17.3.0, Input System 1.18.0 and Test Framework 1.6.0.

## Using the accepted scene

Reopen Unity, allow compilation/import, and press Play in the existing
`Assets/_ColorSlide/Scenes/FeedbackCampaign.unity`. Levels 5 and 6 follow the accepted
four levels. No Apply, regeneration, new scene, setup menu, package change or native
asset creation is required. Other milestone scenes retain their four-level campaigns.

The existing four persistent `LevelDefinition` references remain the scene's authored
base. `CampaignController.CreateCampaign()` appends fresh `LevelData` from
`AdvancedCampaignLevels` when the presentation's existing `GameplayFeedback` flag is
enabled. That flag identifies the accepted FeedbackCampaign presentation. The two new
layouts use the same `LevelData.CreateSession()`, `GridBoard`, `PuzzleSession` and
`LevelPresentation` paths as the SO-backed levels. They are source-authored data, not
new persistent ScriptableObjects or transient ScriptableObject substitutes. Each call
owns fresh arrays; no accepted definition or imported asset is changed.

The runtime campaign has six levels; the serialized `CampaignController.Levels`
property continues to describe its four persistent base definitions. This distinction
also preserves the existing native asset validators. The new tests and authoring tool
validate the complete effective campaign and both new definitions.

### Editor-only Level 5 / 6 QA shortcuts

After the accepted 140/140 Editor test run, temporary level selection was added for
manual QA. In the existing FeedbackCampaign, press Play, focus **Game View**, then
press **5** for Traffic Lock or **6** for Grand Escape. Number-row and numpad keys are
supported, without Shift/Ctrl/Alt/Meta. Play must be unpaused. Pressing the same key
again starts a fresh instance of that level. No Apply, scene edit or additional component is needed.

There was no existing authored-level selection command; `Next` correctly requires a
win. `CampaignSession.StartEditorLevel` shares session preparation with `Next`, staging
the new playing session before committing its index. `CampaignController.StartEditorLevel`
uses the existing `LevelPresentation.Build` and `ActivateReplacement` path: retire the
former hierarchy (cancel drag/burst/contact/gate effects), activate the replacement,
and publish `GameplayChanged`. Timers, HUD, camera fit and feedback therefore follow
normal campaign ownership. It can interrupt a current exit or select from Win/Fail;
earlier levels are not marked won. Level 5 still advances normally to 6 after winning,
and Level 6 completion still offers no Next. The selection is only in memory and a
new Play session starts at Level 1. No PlayerPrefs, assets or progression files are written.

`Editor/CampaignPlayModeShortcuts.cs` subscribes to Input System dynamic updates only
in Play Mode. It requires focused Game View and exactly one active owner in the saved
FeedbackCampaign scene path; other scenes, Inspector/Scene View typing and paused/edit
mode are ignored. Subscription is reset on Play entry, removed on exit, and restored
after a script reload. The input listener lives in the Editor-only assembly; both
selection entry points are inside `#if UNITY_EDITOR`, so Android/player builds contain
neither API nor input listener.

This follow-up changed `CampaignController.cs`, `CampaignSession.cs`, the two standalone

## Puzzle logic and difficulty evidence

The chains below describe the stored witnesses. Alternate legal routes and exit orders
are allowed; a blocked direct route is not a claim that every solution uses that route.

Traffic Lock has several connected tasks. A initially blocks P's upper turn, and P
blocks K's access to the Blue exit. Moving A left lets P rise; K can then align while
P crosses the upper opening. R must leave the narrow right bay to open P's lower exit
route. In the west, A can neither descend through G nor leave through the Green gate.
Temporarily parking A at row 8 opens the Green aperture. Once G has risen, B can use
the vacated left passage to reach the Blue channel; G then exits, A descends, and R
transfers through the lower lane into the freed Green route. These are shared-space
and ordering constraints rather than extra drag distance.

Grand Escape closes the upper Purple crossing. In this witness P parks at `(1,4)` to free K,
but then occupies the cells G needs to rise. P cannot descend past G, and G cannot
park below until C clears. The witness evacuates G temporarily to `(0,0)`, routes P
through the two-row lower transfer, then raises G again. Meanwhile R blocks D's descent,
so R first parks below. D must clear before B can use the right-hand northern route;
R then shifts to `(5,0)` to admit B. Later P at `(4,1)` occupies R's lower swept path,
so P moves up to `(4,2)`, R transfers west, and P returns to row 1 to escape. A must
move away from its own South alignment to open the Green gate, then return. These
coupled parking choices add planning beyond the extra block and board area.

Neither initial layout permits a straight one-action exit for any piece. No solution
action rotates a piece, teleports, moves diagonally, crosses an obstacle, or depends
on a visual bounding-box corner being occupied. Timers allow thinking time; they are
not the source of the difficulty.

### What the numbers mean

One **action** is a positive straight movement of one block along one grid axis to
any reachable resting anchor, or one straight request that exits it. All intermediate
anchors are searched, not only maximum-distance slides. A completed exit counts once,
including its in-board approach. A zero-step blocked request is not a solver action.

One **grid step** is a one-cell translation of the block's anchor. The reported step
totals include its full movement outside the board. A physical held **drag gesture**
can turn between frames, and thus can contain several solver actions. Minimum actions
are not a claim about minimum pointer-downs or minimum grid steps.

| Verification | Actions | Grid steps in stored witness | Search result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Traffic Lock | **21, exact minimum** | 65 | A* proves shortest action count |
| Grand Escape | **32-action witness; minimum in [26,32]** | 91 | Bounded exact search proves lower bound 26; minimum not established |

The final level therefore requires at least five more straight actions than Traffic
(at least 24% more); its stored solution has 52% more actions and 40% more grid travel.
The dependency tests also exercise its closed bypass and mutual parking obstruction.
These are logical measurements, not a substitute for human difficulty/playtesting.
The witness includes choices that need not be optimal; no exact minimum for Grand is claimed.
Relative to independent-piece shortest paths, Traffic needs one extra action and Grand
needs at least four: interactions impose a larger verified planning cost, beyond its
solo obstacle detours and additional block.

### Search implementation, bounds and reproduction

`Editor/PuzzleSolver.cs` is authoring-only. It stores each piece's anchor/removal in
a compact deterministic key and expands legal directions in a fixed order. It calls
the actual `GridBoard.MoveWithExits` for every successor, including full-footprint
occupancy, obstacles, color matching, aperture fit and exit sweep. Undo uses the real
cardinal movement API; another direction starts from the same state.

The heuristic sums exact independent-piece action distances to an exit, with the
same obstacles and gates but other pieces removed. This is admissible and consistent:
one real action moves only one piece and cannot improve its solo distance by more than
one. A* weight 1 proves a minimum on reaching the goal. Weight 2 is a bounded witness
search and does not prove optimality. The stable heap sorts score, heuristic, then
insertion order. Best costs allow reopening; there is no random seed or generated layout.

Measured in the standalone Unity Mono runtime on this workstation:

| Search | Expansion / discovery caps | Expanded | Unique states | Time | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Traffic, weight 1 | 5,000 / 20,000 | 368 | 6,955 | 25 ms | shortest 21 |
| Grand, weight 2 | 5,000 / 20,000 | 403 | 3,825 | 22 ms | legal 32-action witness |
| Grand, weight 1 | 300,000 / 1,500,000 | 202,159 | 1,500,000 | 16,760 ms | discovery cap; frontier lower bound 26 |

The exact Grand search stops at its explicit memory/state budget. Unexpanded and
not-yet-inserted successors at that point have admissible cost at least 26. A failed
search is not treated as an unsolvable puzzle; its independent stored witness solves
it. Times depend on hardware/JIT/cache. Expensive exact Grand search is never in the
ordinary test suite, runtime, import callbacks or scene setup.

## Presentation and touch considerations

`BoardFraming` builds a conservative envelope for tiles, obstacles, glowing gate
jambs, whole departing shapes and fragments at both extreme matching gate alignments.
Fragment bounds use the continuous easing/arc ranges and a rotation-independent cube
radius, rather than relying only on sampled instants. Frame fitting projects all eight
envelope corners onto the actual camera's right/up axes.

The gameplay rectangle respects `Screen.safeArea`. It reserves 316 Canvas reference
pixels at the top (accepted 280-pixel HUD, 12-pixel offset, 24-pixel separation), 72 at
the bottom and 32 at each side. Scaling matches the accepted 1080×1920 CanvasScaler's
0.5 width/height match. The complete presentation envelope is centered in this region.
Orthographic size is the larger of the required fit and the existing aspect-based
reference size. Camera translation is in its image plane, preserving pitch, projection,
depth and clipping distances. No level-ID offsets or model scaling are used.

`PortraitCameraFit` applies this only to boards wider than six or taller than eight.
The original four retain their old origin, size rule and camera position. Framing does
not mutate the camera during EditMode construction/validation. It follows resolution,
aspect and safe-area changes during Play, restores the original camera position when
there is no large board, and restores it on camera disable. Moving the camera leaves
drag origins, cell coordinates, colliders and cached exit/burst world positions stable.

Measured at 1080px width, with 96px top and 64px bottom safe-area exclusions:

| Resolution | Traffic ortho size | Traffic cell pitch X / projected Y | Grand ortho size | Grand cell pitch X / projected Y |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1080×1920 (9:16) | 6.5314 | 99.95 / 89.40 px | 7.8035 | 83.65 / 74.82 px |
| 1080×2340 (9:19.5) | 8.0126 | 99.29 / 88.81 px | 9.5732 | 83.11 / 74.33 px |
| 1080×2400 (9:20) | 8.2254 | 99.20 / 88.73 px | 9.8275 | 83.03 / 74.27 px |

These are projected grid pitches, not promised physical touch-target dimensions.
Grand trades some selection area for its wider board and fully visible exits/bursts;
single cells may feel small on a physically narrow device. The existing per-cell
BoxColliders and drag thresholds are preserved, including the selectable L footprint
and its unoccupied corner. Real touch QA is required; no invisible hit padding, zoom,
camera-angle change or misleading large-target claim was introduced.
